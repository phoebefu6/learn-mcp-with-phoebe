## Never-cut beats (the session fails without these)

1. **The four numbers + "the question changed."** The point is not the figures; it is that adoption is now assumed, so decisions are "which/from where/what order."
2. **The five-point listing test.** This is the reusable skill of the session. Walk all five, slowly.
3. **The build-vs-buy-vs-wait matrix**, especially "build on proprietary data." This is the strategic spine of a5.
4. **Demo 1's scored answer** - reject MegaCRM Bridge out loud and say why. It proves the method works with no technical input.

## Cuts if running long

- Drop the aggregators/enterprise-catalog self-study card - it is reading and a5 revisits it.
- Shorten Part 1 to just the four numbers; skip the "honest numbers" nuance if time is tight (but mention "these move fast").
- Make Demo 2 fully take-home rather than starting it live.
- Never cut the five-point test or the matrix - cut narration elsewhere.

## Three likely audience questions + answers

**Q1: "Are these numbers real, and will they hold?"**
A: Treat them as direction, not precision. ~97M monthly SDK downloads and ~10k servers are momentum signals that move every quarter; the durable claim is qualitative - every major vendor now speaks MCP, so it has crossed from promising to assumed. Before any board deck, pull the current figure rather than reusing mine.

**Q2: "If a server is in the official registry, is it safe to use?"**
A: No - a listing means it exists and passed a basic moderation bar, not that it is right for you. It is the app-store analogy: being in the store is not an endorsement. That is exactly why the five-point test exists, and why mature organizations keep a private, vetted allow-list drawn from the public registry.

**Q3: "How do we decide build vs buy without an engineering background?"**
A: Two questions you can answer without engineering: how sensitive is the data, and how differentiating is the capability? Sensitive plus differentiating means build and own it; low on both means buy a maintained server or wait. Put your top systems on the matrix and the policy falls out - then hand the "build" list to your data team.
