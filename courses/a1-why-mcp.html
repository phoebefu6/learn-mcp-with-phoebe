Part 1 · covers official "What is MCP", DLAI "Why MCP"

The integration tax you are already paying 8 min live

Here is the budget problem hiding inside every AI initiative. Each AI application that should reach a business system needs a connector - and before MCP, every connector was custom. Three AI apps times three systems is nine builds. Ten apps times twenty systems is two hundred. Each one has its own auth, its own error handling, its own breakage schedule, and its own maintenance line in somebody's budget. That multiplication - M apps x N systems - is the integration tax, and it grows faster than any headcount plan.

LiveWhy every AI pilot stalls at integration4 min▶

The demo is always easy: the model answers beautifully about whatever you paste into the chat window. The pilot dies the day someone asks it to answer from your systems - the CRM, the warehouse, the ticket queue. That is when a two-week pilot becomes a two-quarter integration project, because every connection had to be built by hand, approved by security, and maintained forever.

  • The demo-to-production gap is an integration gap, not a model gap. The model was never the blocker.
  • Every custom connector is a permanent liability: its own credentials, its own failure modes, its own owner who eventually changes teams.
  • Vendors could not save you: before MCP, an AI app could only reach the systems its vendor chose to integrate, on the vendor's release schedule.
Real world

The pilot that stalled at the warehouse door. A retail group ran a successful assistant pilot on uploaded spreadsheets. Moving it to live sales data meant a bespoke integration between the AI vendor's plugin format and their warehouse - quoted at four months. By the time it shipped, the vendor had changed plugin formats. With MCP the same connection is one server their data team owns, and it works with every MCP-speaking app they adopt afterwards - including ones that did not exist when the server was written.

LiveThe connector team cost3 min▶

Put a price on the grid. A bespoke connector typically costs weeks of engineering to build and a steady fraction of an engineer to keep alive - auth rotations, API changes on either side, monitoring, incident response. Multiply by the number of pairs and you get the quiet headcount sink many organizations call "the platform team's plugin backlog".

  • Build cost: each pair needs discovery, auth design, error handling, and security review - none of it reusable for the next pair.
  • Carry cost: connectors break when either side updates. M x N connectors means M x N independent breakage schedules.
  • Opportunity cost: every quarter spent on glue code is a quarter not spent on the use cases the glue was for.

MCP does not make integration free - someone still writes and runs each server. It makes integration additive instead of multiplicative, and moves the spend from per-pair glue to per-system capability your whole AI portfolio shares.

Part 2 · covers official "What is MCP" governance + adoption

The USB-C move 8 min live

Industries solve multiplication problems the same way every time: standardize the plug, not the devices. USB-C did it for cables. HTTP did it for the web - any browser, any website, one protocol. MCP is the same move for AI: any AI app, any tool or data source, one protocol in the middle. Anthropic shipped it as an open standard in November 2024, and the rest of the industry did something rare - they adopted a competitor's standard, because the alternative was rebuilding the same grid separately.

LiveRuntime discovery - the part USB-C analogies miss4 min▶

The standard plug is only half the story. The sharper half is runtime discovery: when an MCP-speaking app connects to a server, it asks "what can you do?" and the server describes its own capabilities on the spot. The app was never programmed to know about your CRM - it learns at connection time.

  • New capabilities without vendor releases: your team ships a new server, and every AI app in the building can use it the same day. No waiting on Anthropic, OpenAI, or Microsoft to "support" your system.
  • Precedent - the browser: websites do not ship as browser updates. HTTP plus discovery is why the web scaled; MCP applies the same logic to AI capabilities.
  • Precedent - USB-C: the port negotiates what the device can do (charge, display, data) when you plug in. MCP servers negotiate the same way.

For a leader, this is the headline: connecting AI to a system becomes configuration, not a development project. That is what collapses the timeline from quarters to days.

Self-studyWho owns it now - governance you can defend in a board pack3 min read▶

Standards bets fail two ways: the standard loses, or the owner weaponizes it. MCP has answered both. On adoption: OpenAI (March 2025), Google DeepMind (April 2025), Microsoft across Copilot and VS Code, plus AI-native tools like Cursor. On ownership: in December 2025 Anthropic donated MCP to the Agentic AI Foundation under the Linux Foundation, with OpenAI and Block as co-founders.

  • Neutral governance: changes flow through a public proposal process, not one vendor's roadmap. No single company can move the standard against the ecosystem.
  • Scale signals: roughly 97 million monthly SDK downloads and about 10,000 active servers, with an official registry running since September 2025.
  • What this means for risk: adopting MCP is not a bet on Anthropic. It is a bet on the same class of asset as HTTP or Kubernetes - open, foundation-governed, multi-vendor.
Part 3 · the leadership takeaway

What this means for your org 6 min live

If the plug is standard, the money moves. Integration budget shifts away from glue code - the per-pair connectors nobody loves - and toward two things worth funding deliberately: capability (which systems should AI reach, exposed as well-designed servers) and governance (who may connect what to what, with which permissions). The organizations getting value from MCP in 2026 are not the ones with the most servers; they are the ones who made the build-once, govern-always trade early.

LiveThree signals MCP matters to you now4 min▶

Not every organization needs to move this quarter. These three signals say you do:

  • 1 · You run more than one AI app. Claude in one team, Copilot in another, Cursor in engineering - the moment M is greater than one, the multiplication starts. One MCP server per system serves all of them.
  • 2 · Your AI pilots keep asking for the same data. If three pilots each requested warehouse access separately, you are paying the tax three times. That is one Daybreak-style server your data team should own.
  • 3 · People are already connecting things without you. MCP servers are easy to adopt - which means enthusiastic employees may be wiring AI apps to systems today, with no inventory and no policy. The governance conversation is cheaper to have before the sprawl (a4 and a5 give you the playbook).

Questions to ask your teams this week: Which AI apps are in use, officially and unofficially? Which systems have they been connected to? Who approved those connections? The answers are the raw material for the exercise below.

Demo 1 of 2

Count your own N x M ★ 8 min · pen and paper

The multiplication is abstract until it is your multiplication. Take five minutes and put real names in the grid - this number is the business case for everything else in this course.

List the AI apps in use in your organization - official and tolerated. Claude, Copilot, Cursor, ChatGPT, internal chatbots. That is your M.

List the systems AI should reach to be genuinely useful: CRM, warehouse, ticketing, docs, HR, finance. Be honest, not exhaustive - the top eight will do. That is your N.

Multiply. M x N is the number of custom connectors the old world required. Write it down next to M + N, the MCP world's number.

Price the gap. Estimate each custom connector at several engineer-weeks to build plus ongoing upkeep. The difference between M x N and M + N, in engineer-weeks, is your integration tax - the number to open your next platform-budget conversation with.

Keep the worksheet You will reuse this list in a3 (which servers to buy vs build) and a5 (rollout sequencing). The three columns worth keeping: system name, business value if AI could reach it, and who owns the data.
Demo 2 of 2

The elevator brief ★ 6 min · write it in your own words

You will be asked "what is this MCP thing?" by someone senior, probably soon, probably in a hallway. Draft your three-sentence answer now: one sentence on what it is, one on why it won, one on what you propose to do about it. Then compare with the model answer below - but yours, in your voice, is the one that will land.

LiveA model answer to steal from3 min▶

Sentence 1 - what it is: "MCP is an open standard that lets any AI app connect to any of our systems through one common plug, instead of a custom integration for every pairing."

Sentence 2 - why it won: "Anthropic released it in late 2024, OpenAI, Google, and Microsoft all adopted it within months, and it now sits under the Linux Foundation - so it is neutral infrastructure, not a vendor bet."

Sentence 3 - what we do: "I want us to inventory what is already connected, pick two systems where AI access has clear value, and stand up governed connections there first."

Notice what the answer avoids: protocol mechanics, primitive names, JSON. The architecture vocabulary arrives in a2 - and only the parts a leader actually needs.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · Your org runs 5 AI apps and wants them reaching 12 systems. What does MCP change about the integration count?

The tax is multiplicative (M x N), the fix is additive (M + N). Each app implements MCP once, each system gets one server once, and every combination works. Someone still builds and runs each server - the work does not vanish, it stops multiplying.

2 · Who governs MCP in 2026?

Anthropic created MCP (Nov 2024) but donated it in December 2025. Neutral, foundation-based governance with rivals as co-founders is exactly what makes MCP defensible as an infrastructure bet rather than a vendor bet.

3 · What does runtime discovery buy your organization?

MCP apps ask a server "what can you do?" when they connect. Your team ships a new server and every MCP-speaking app can use it immediately - connecting AI to a system becomes configuration, not a development project waiting on a vendor.
